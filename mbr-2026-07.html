Industry-Life Sciences Deep Dive — 18.1% of July Volume

LSC vs Overall — Bug Rate & Investigation Rate

Bug Inflow by Product — July (Total: 124)

Life Sciences is the single biggest driver of July volume, bugs, and investigations. 607 cases in July (18.1% of total), up 32% from June and 74% from April. It accounts for 66 of 124 bugs (53%) — a bug rate of 10.9% versus the overall rate of 3.7%. It also drives 129 of 251 investigations (51.2%). These 129 investigation-linked cases are the main reason India TTR stays elevated — they take on average 13–15 days to close because the team has to wait for engineering to respond. The top accounts inside LSC: Takeda Pharmaceuticals (103 cases, 3.1% of all July inflow — volume doubled from 73 in June), Deloitte Global (66 cases, 20 S1s), and Pfizer (64 cases, all S3, still open). This concentration points to a product-level problem that engineering needs to own, not just support.

Key Observations for MBR
1. Industry-Life Sciences keeps growing — and it is driving bugs and investigations too.
607 cases in July, up 32% from June and 74% from April. This one product is now 18.1% of all inflow. More importantly, it generated 66 bugs (53% of all July bugs) and 129 investigations (51% of all July investigations). Bug rate is 10.9% vs 3.7% overall. This is a product quality issue — support is handling the symptoms but engineering needs to address the root cause.
2. Takeda volume doubled in one month (73 → 144 cases).
Takeda is now the highest-volume account at 4.3% of all July inflow. 102 of their 144 cases are Sev2, all handled in India. 23 cases are still open. This level of acceleration from a single account in one month needs a direct conversation with the account team.
3. India TTR is 10.39 days — the reason is engineering dependency, not slow handling.
Standard cases (no Bug, no GUS) close in 4–7 days in India — within target. But cases with a Bug attached take 11.8 days on average, and cases with a GUS work item take 13.7 days. India has 94 GUS-linked and 45 Bug-linked closed cases this month, mostly driven by Life Sciences. These cases wait on engineering response before they can close. The fix is faster engineering turnaround on Life Sciences investigations.
4. Deloitte group: 116 cases combined, 17 Sev1s, three consecutive months of increase.
Deloitte Global Services (72 cases, 17 S1) plus Deloitte Consulting LLP (44 cases) = 3.5% of all July volume. S1 cases from Deloitte Global have gone 4 → 10 → 17 over three months. This is not random — it points to a common underlying issue across their Salesforce implementations. A coordinated account review across both Deloitte entities is needed.
5. Four accounts raised Sev1 cases for the first time in July.
McDonald's (7 S1), N28 Technologies (6 S1), London Business School (5 S1), and TATA Motors (5 S1) all had zero Sev1 cases in May and June. Four new accounts appearing together in the same month is unusual and suggests a shared trigger — possibly a recent platform release or a migration issue.
6. Several metrics moved in the right direction in July.
EMEA TTR improved for the third straight month, reaching 6.94 days. Closures were the highest in three months at 3,485. Sev1 fell 9.3% overall, led by India recovering from June's spike. Escalation % dropped to 0.5%. Throughput reached 1.93. Chat Mix continued climbing (14.7% → 15.8% → 16.6%), showing steady adoption. The operational foundation is solid — the main risks are concentrated in Life Sciences and a handful of accounts.
