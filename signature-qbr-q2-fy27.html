Section 6 — Executive Summary
Portfolio Inflow & Service Health
Scale, customer outcomes, and operational stability
IN
Inflow: QoQ Normalization with Strong YoY Growth
Q2 inflow was 2,857 cases, down 4.2% QoQ but up 23% YoY. Closures reached 2,852, matching 99.8% of inflow. Signature share rose to 29.0% and reached 30.3% in July, showing that the portfolio remains strategically important as demand shifts toward complex premium support.
CX
Service Health: Strong CSAT and Escalation Control
CSAT remained strong at 4.88, escalation improved to 0.4%, and SLO miss held at 0.1%. Sev1 volume declined 27% QoQ, with 96% resolved. Investigation rate improved to 13.0% and bug rate to 8.3%, both lower QoQ and YoY despite materially higher annual inflow.
Account Story
Deployment maturity, growth, and support outcomes
LS
Life Sciences Accounts Are Driving Portfolio Expansion
Six of eight net-new accounts are Life Sciences or Pharma. Pfizer volume eased 26% QoQ as its initial deployment stabilized, while Deloitte grew 30%. Mylan and other new entrants show healthy adoption demand. The account pattern points to product rollout and implementation scale, not broad service deterioration.
NEW
Net-New Accounts Added 344 Q2 Cases
Eight accounts entered the top-volume cohort, led by Mylan, Apsen, Moderna, and Metlen. Six are Life Sciences or Pharma and contributed 274 cases, representing 80% of net-new account volume. This is a concentrated adoption signal: demand is expanding through new product deployments, requiring deliberate onboarding and early-life support coverage.
AC
Growth Accounts Show Healthy Resolution Outcomes
Claro grew 29% QoQ with only 4% bug dependency. BT Consumer grew 59% as EPC usage ramped, while engineering dependency remained low. Airtel recorded the highest Sev1 volume, but all cases were resolved in 2.1 days on average with no GUS investigations or confirmed bugs.
Product Story
Where demand is growing and where the portfolio is stabilizing
LS
Life Sciences Is the Primary Growth Engine
Life Sciences volume increased 40% QoQ to 439 cases, driven by new deployments and account onboarding. Its Sev1 increase from 1 to 10 should be read in the context of rapid adoption across six new accounts rather than as a mature-product quality regression.
RC
Revenue Cloud Mix Is Moving Toward Newer Capabilities
Revenue CPQ increased 10% QoQ, while RC Core Product Configuration grew 9% QoQ and 94% YoY. CPQ/Order Management and OmniStudio volumes eased, and together drove 58% of the Sev1 decline, indicating improving stability in established products.
Key Product & Engineering Focus
Prioritize joint support where adoption complexity and engineering dependency intersect
1
Life Sciences Requires Scale Support
TTR is at the 10-day target but above last year as the new Life Sciences product and account wave increases case complexity. Volume grew 40% QoQ and six of eight new accounts are Life Sciences or Pharma. Maintain joint CS, engineering, and product-team coverage through the adoption ramp.
2
Retail & Consumer Goods Needs Faster Engineering Turnaround
Sev1 volume improved, but TTR remains elevated at 15.3 days with a 50% investigation rate and 29% bug rate. The support signal is concentrated in engineering resolution time rather than inflow. Strengthen R&D triage cadence and product-team ownership.
3
Revenue Cloud Core Needs Adoption-Stage Coverage
RC Core Product Configuration grew 9% QoQ and 94% YoY as demand shifts toward newer capabilities. Investigation dependency reflects implementation and configuration complexity during adoption. Keep product specialists aligned with support and engineering as usage scales.
4
Investigation and Bug Rates Should Guide Capacity
Portfolio investigation rate improved to 13.0% and bug rate to 8.3%, both better QoQ and YoY. Continue product-level tracking to separate adoption-driven demand from systemic quality issues and direct engineering capacity to the highest-dependency products.
