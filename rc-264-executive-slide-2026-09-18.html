Speaker Notes — Executive Summary

Opening: Release 264 (Winter '27) closed its first 7 days with 54 IR cases from 490 RC inflow — a 10.6% IR rate. While this is a clear step back from 262's exceptional 2.8%, it remains meaningfully better than the 258/260 baseline of ~12%. Two root causes account for the majority of volume.

Top drivers: Bug W-24059609 (Net Unit Price null in Amendment) drove 11 cases in Product Configurator, and W-24096152 (PCM Index Rebuild Failure) drove 8 cases in Product Catalog Management. Together, these two bugs represent ~35% of all 264 IR cases. Fixes are already in progress — status to be confirmed at bridge call.

Account impact: Neocol is the only account appearing in top-5 impact across all four releases, with 2 unique bugs in 264. Lutron Electronics was consistently impacted in 258 and 260 but has not reappeared in 262 or 264 — a positive signal for that customer's environment stability.

Trend context: 262 (Summer '26) was an outlier — 12 IR from 427 inflow — driven by a highly focused regression scope. 264 reverted toward prior norms. The 260 unique-bug spike (35 bugs) was the broadest regression, but IR volume was only marginally higher than 258, showing that broad shallow regressions can be less damaging than a few deep ones.

Ask / Next steps: (1) Confirm W-24059609 and W-24096152 fix ETA and backport plan. (2) Review whether Neocol needs proactive outreach given 4-release impact streak. (3) Use 262 playbook (tight pre-SB1 regression scope) as model for 266 planning.
