🔍 Pattern Analysis

264 — Flat, sustained plateau (D3–D7) Unlike 258/260 which had one sharp spike (D4) then dropped, 264 runs at 11–13/day from D3 through D5, still at 9 on D7. No single "peak day" — more like a rolling wave. This suggests multiple org cohorts hitting different bugs at different times, not one batch event.
258 + 260 both spiked on D4 — same pattern D4 is consistently the highest-volume day for 258 (32) and 260 (28). This is likely the "business week Monday" effect: SB1 hits Friday/weekend, engineering triage starts Monday, customers test on Monday, cases flood in D4. 264 SB1 was a Friday too but didn't spike the same way.
262 — Uniquely flat and low across all 7 days Peak was only 5 on D3. Every other day was ≤3. The underlying IR rate was genuinely low — not a reporting artifact. Confirms the 2.8% IR rate as a structural improvement, not a timing anomaly.
264 D7 = 9 vs 258 D7 = 2, 260 D7 = 5 264 is still trending high at end of day 7. Prior releases were winding down (2–5 cases). Combined with week 2 showing 49 more cases, this confirms 264 had a longer tail — the issue wasn't contained within the first 7 days.
